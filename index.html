Note No. YX‑2026‑001 Student Voice Note · Not Legal Tender

Section One

The proposals

Three ideas worth pursuing, and how I would go about proposing them. None of this happens without discussion — each card is honest about what still needs approval, and what's already confirmed.

The problem

Students need a clearer way to raise ideas, questions and concerns — and to know whether anything happened afterwards. A note left in a box that's never opened doesn't fix that.

The proposal

A physical Student Voice Board, where students leave questions, suggestions, feedback and issues on sticky notes — ideas, questions, school issues, suggestions, or things students want improved. Council reviews submissions regularly and posts a response on the board itself, so the board shows both what was asked and what happened next.

How it would work

  1. Leave a note
  2. Review
  3. Respond
  4. Update the board

Requires school approval to set up and run.

Where it stands now

Homework Club currently gives students a quiet place and time to complete homework while waiting for parents or siblings.

What I'd propose instead

A redesigned version built around daily student-led learning sessions — a different presenter and topic each day, with presenters chosen through interviews.

  • At least 30 minutes of presentation
  • A quiz afterwards
  • A task to demonstrate understanding
  • House points proposed as an incentive, subject to school approval

How it would work

  1. Present
  2. Quiz
  3. Task
  4. Prove understanding

This is a proposal to redesign Homework Club, not a promise that it will replace it — subject to school approval.

A closer look

Your idea shouldn't disappear into a box.

The point of the Student Voice Board is a visible loop: what was raised, whether it's been discussed, what's possible, whether it happened — and if it can't, why. Submitting something never guarantees it's accepted; the promise is a more transparent process, not a yes to everything.
